## Methods

### Models and predictions
EfficientNet-B0 and EfficientNet-B4 were evaluated on the same 5,452-sample test split. B0 used 224 × 224 input images, while B4 used 380 × 380 images. Both evaluation pipelines used normalization and tensor conversion.

### Error-detection scores
Prediction uncertainty was defined as one minus the model's maximum predicted class probability. Inter-model disagreement was defined as a binary indicator of whether B0 and B4 predicted different classes. The combined score was a weighted sum of uncertainty and disagreement:

\[
S = \alpha(1-C) + (1-\alpha)D
\]

where \(C\) is maximum class confidence, \(D\) is the disagreement indicator, and \(\alpha\) is selected on the validation set. Validation AUROC was used to select the weight. The selected values were 0.78 for B0 and 0.57 for B4.

### Evaluation
Error detection was evaluated using AUROC, AUPRC, and precision, recall, and F1 among the top 20% of predictions ranked by error score. Risk–coverage curves were also generated by retaining predictions in ascending error-score order and calculating cumulative error rate as coverage increased. Lower area under the risk–coverage curve (AURC) indicates lower error risk among retained predictions.

### Data splitting and limitations
The test set contained 5,452 samples. The dataset was divided into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) subsets using two-stage stratified random splitting by diagnosis class, with a fixed random seed. The split was performed at the image-row level; patient- and lesion-level independence was not established. Sample identifiers therefore do not demonstrate patient-level independence. Results should be interpreted as internal test-split findings rather than evidence of generalization to independent patients.
